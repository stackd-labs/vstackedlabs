01

Intake automation

5 prompts

Systematize lead qualification, intake forms, auto-responses, and discovery calls. Replace hours of manual intake work with repeatable AI-generated outputs.

You are an intake assistant for [your business name], a [type of business]. When a potential client reaches out, ask them 5 questions to determine fit, understand budget, and clarify timeline. Tone: warm but efficient. Our ideal client is [describe in 1–2 sentences]. Generate the 5 intake questions now.

Deploy: Paste output directly into Typeform, Notion form, or your website contact page.

Write a warm, professional auto-reply for new inquiries to [business name]. (1) Confirm receipt, (2) set response time expectations [X hours/days], (3) briefly state what we do, (4) share a booking link [Calendly URL]. Under 120 words. Tone: [friendly & professional / bold & direct / warm & personal].

Deploy: Drop into Gmail canned responses, HoneyBook, or your CRM auto-reply configuration.

Write a kind but clear email to a prospect who is not a fit for [business name] right now. Reason: [budget too low / wrong service type / outside scope]. Decline without burning the relationship, wish them well, and optionally redirect to [a resource, referral, or lower-tier option]. Under 100 words.

Note: Redirect to your lowest-ticket offer or a referral partner — keeps the relationship warm for future re-engagement.

Create a client intake form for [business name] that collects: contact info, project or service details, budget range, timeline, referral source, and special notes. Format as labeled questions with field types (short text, dropdown, paragraph). Our services: [list 2–3 services].

Deploy: Build directly from this output in Typeform, JotForm, or Notion — copy, paste, publish.

Write a 15-minute discovery call script for [business name]: (1) warm intro + agenda (2 min), (2) 4 key questions about their situation (6 min), (3) brief solution overview (4 min), (4) next steps + close (3 min). Main offer: [describe primary service]. Call goal: [book project / qualify for proposal / enroll].

Cadence: Print before every call. Update iteratively as you identify which questions drive the best conversations.

02

Client communication

5 prompts

Eliminate friction in difficult client emails. Follow-ups, scope enforcement, payment collection, status updates, and issue management — handled in under 60 seconds.

Write a short, warm follow-up to a client I have not heard from in [X days/weeks]. Context: [describe where the project or conversation left off]. Should feel human, not robotic. Check in, offer help, leave one clear next step. Under 80 words.

Use when: A client goes quiet mid-project. Re-opens the conversation naturally without applying pressure.

Write a professional, firm but kind email to a client requesting work outside our original scope. Original scope: [describe what was agreed]. New requests: [describe the additions]. Acknowledge their needs, redirect to the original agreement, and offer to quote the additional work separately. Tone: respectful, not defensive.

Note: Always reference the original contract or SOW in this email. Documentation is your leverage.

Write 3 versions of a payment reminder for invoice #[number] totaling [$amount], due [date], for [client name]. Version 1: friendly nudge (3 days late). Version 2: firm and direct (10 days late). Version 3: final notice with late fee warning (21 days late). Under 100 words each.

Deploy: Save all 3 as email templates with calendar reminders — remove this from your mental load entirely.

Write a short client-facing project update. Project: [name]. Completed this week: [2–3 items]. Next steps: [1–2 items]. Blockers or items needed from client: [list or "none"]. Scannable with bullet points. Close with a clear ask if needed. Professional but conversational.

Note: Consistent updates prevent client anxiety — and anxious clients cause scope creep. Send even when progress feels small.

Write an honest, professional email about a delay or issue. Situation: [describe what happened]. New timeline: [date or range]. Take responsibility without over-apologizing. Briefly explain what happened. Give confidence the situation is resolved. Under 150 words.

Principle: Lead with the new timeline, not the apology. Clients want to know what happens next.

03

Proposal drafting

5 prompts

Compress proposal turnaround from hours to minutes. Full proposals, SOWs, value-framed pricing, and follow-up sequences engineered to convert.

Write a professional proposal for: Client: [name]. What they need: [describe project/service]. What I'm offering: [deliverables + timeline]. Investment: [price or range]. Include: overview, what's included, what's not included, timeline, investment, next steps. Tone: confident and credible, not salesy.

Deploy: Paste into HoneyBook, PandaDoc, or Notion for a client-ready proposal.

Write a confident pricing explanation for my proposal. Service: [describe]. Price: [$X]. Value the client receives: [outcomes/results]. Frame around value and results, not hours or tasks. Do not apologize for the price. 2–3 short paragraphs.

Principle: Clients buy outcomes, not line items. This section alone reduces price objections significantly.

Create a statement of work for: Client: [name]. Project: [title]. Deliverables: [list each]. Timeline: [start to end date]. Revisions: [X rounds]. Out of scope: [list exclusions]. Numbered sections, plain language, legally intentional.

Critical: The out-of-scope section prevents 80% of scope creep conversations. Never omit it.

Write a 3-email follow-up sequence for a prospect who received a proposal but has not responded. Email 1 (Day 3): gentle check-in. Email 2 (Day 7): add value, address a likely objection. Email 3 (Day 14): final nudge with soft urgency around availability. Proposal was for [service] at [$price]. Under 90 words each.

Data: Most proposal conversions happen on follow-up 2 or 3. Automate this and remove it from your mental load.

A client asked me to lower my price from [$X]. They said: [quote or paraphrase]. Write a confident response that holds the price while offering a structured alternative — either reduced scope or a payment plan. Do not discount without reducing scope. Tone: understanding but firm.

Principle: Offer to reduce scope before reducing price — protects your rate and sets the right precedent.

04

Weekly planning

5 prompts

Deploy Claude as a dedicated strategic thinking partner. Weekly kickoffs, prioritization, end-of-week reviews, decision frameworks, and revenue modeling — consistently and in minutes.

Help me plan my week. Top 3 priorities: [list]. Carryover tasks: [list]. Scheduled commitments: [meetings, deadlines]. Energy level: [high / medium / low]. Create a realistic daily plan Mon–Fri. Flag anything overloaded or unrealistic.

Cadence: Run every Monday. 3 minutes of structured planning eliminates hours of reactive spinning throughout the week.

I'm a business owner with too many items on my plate. Full task list: [dump everything here]. Current biggest bottleneck or goal: [describe]. Rank by business impact and urgency. Give me the top 3 to execute first with reasoning. Flag what to delegate, delete, or defer.

Use when: Overwhelmed by volume. Claude excels at surfacing the highest-leverage action in a noisy list.

End-of-week review. Accomplished: [list]. Did not complete: [list]. What felt draining: [note]. What felt energizing: [note]. Identify one behavioral pattern to address and set 3 clear priorities for next week. Be direct and concise.

Cadence: Run on Friday, not Monday. The pattern recognition compounds over weeks — your most underrated business tool.

I'm deciding between: Option A: [describe] and Option B: [describe]. Context: [current situation, goals, constraints]. Analyze trade-offs across: short-term revenue, long-term positioning, time cost, execution risk, and goal alignment. Provide a direct recommendation with reasoning. Do not hedge.

Use before: Any decision you have been sitting on for more than 3 days. The framework surfaces the obvious answer.

Monthly revenue target: [$X]. Current offers and prices: [list each with price]. Generate 3 distinct offer combinations that reach this target. Identify: which path requires the fewest transactions, which is most realistic at 10–20% conversion, and which has the highest margin efficiency.

Cadence: Run at the start of each month. Seeing the math eliminates emotional friction around revenue goals.

05

Operations & SOPs

5 prompts

Build operational infrastructure. Process documentation, role specs, client onboarding systems, policy drafting, and quarterly audits — the foundation that makes delegation possible.

Write a step-by-step SOP for: [process — e.g. client onboarding, complaint handling, order fulfillment]. Business: [name]. Executing role: [role title]. Include: purpose, required tools, numbered steps, common failure points to avoid, and a completion checklist.

Priority: Document your top 3–5 recurring processes first. That is where delegation and automation become possible.

Write a contractor or job description for: Role: [title]. Business: [name]. Weekly responsibilities: [3–5 tasks]. Required skills: [list]. Hours per week: [X]. Compensation: [rate or range]. Include a "definition of success at 90 days" section. Tone: founder-direct, not corporate HR.

Post on: LinkedIn, Contra, or Toptal — selection depends on role type and contractor vs. employee status.

Create a new client onboarding checklist for [business name], service: [describe]. Cover four phases: pre-start (contract, payment, intake form), Day 1 (welcome email, portal access, intro call), Week 1 (kickoff, deliverable timeline), and ongoing cadence. Format as checklist with owner labels (Me / Client).

Impact: Structured onboarding reduces client anxiety and eliminates 80% of scope creep before it begins.

Draft professional business policies for [business name] covering: (1) Refund policy, (2) Revision rounds and process, (3) Late payment fees and timeline, (4) Project pause and cancellation terms. Plain language, firm tone. 3–5 sentences per section. These will be embedded in proposals and contracts.

Deploy: Embed in every proposal before signature. Ambiguity is the primary driver of client disputes.

Audit my business operations. Monthly revenue: [$X]. Active clients: [#]. Highest time-cost tasks: [list]. Manual processes that could be automated: [list]. Processes not yet documented: [list]. Identify the top 3 operational gaps and produce a prioritized action plan with timelines. Be direct.

Cadence: Run quarterly. Recurring gaps across audits are your highest-cost inefficiencies — fix those first.
